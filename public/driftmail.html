Driftmail

The tide is turning…

Driftmail keeps your island on your account, so it's there whenever you come back.

Sign in or create an account to wash up on your island.

  1. Press Write a letter and write to whoever finds it.
  2. Pick a direction and how hard to throw. The hint tells you which way the current runs.
  3. Throw it, then come back later: bottles wash up as the tide goes out.

                              How the sea works

                              You're alone on your island. The only way to reach anyone is a letter in a bottle, and nobody can choose who finds it.

                              Where a bottle goes depends on which way you throw it, how hard, the time of day and the tide. The current runs north at dawn, east at midday, south at dusk and west at night, and an outgoing tide carries a bottle much further than an incoming one. Most bottles reach islands near yours.

                              A bottle washes up on the beach facing where it came from. To answer one, throw your reply back the way it came: aimed well, it may find its way home.

                              Signing a letter is up to you. Even unsigned, a letter is in its writer's own hand and carries their mark in the wax, so you may come to recognise someone without ever learning their name.

                              Now and then the whole sea changes for a while: a storm, a spring tide, a shoal of fish, wreckage from some far-off ship, and rarer things: a red tide, a comet, a great ebb, a fog bank, currents that run backwards. The sea gives slight signs the tide before, if you're watching.

                              The tide leaves driftwood, rope, clay and more, and you can gather leaves, clay, sand and stones inland once a tide. Build a shack in three stages to keep more letters, and a kiln, a drying rack and an ink press to make your own bottles, paper and ink. Plant the seeds from your crate, and water them: crops only grow while watered.

                              You can fish once a tide. Read the water first: one spot is lively each tide, and the water shows which. Watch the float: little dips are only nibbles, so strike when it goes right under. Then the fish pulls, and you answer each pull: lean against it when it runs left or right, and give it line when it dives. Dawn, dusk, a rising tide and a berry for bait all help.

                              Most things need building first. Garden beds let you plant; a workshop lets you make hooks, spears, traps, corks, glue and oil; a writing desk (inside the shack) lets you copy pages, draw and keep a chart.

                              A bottle can carry one thing with the letter: a curio, seeds, a drawing or a map of who writes to you. Jars carry three; boxes and barrels, launched from a dock, carry far more but can break in a storm. Long drifts let water in and blur the ink, unless the bottle is double-corked.

                              Mark the letters you find kind, useful or rude. The sea remembers, and a writer it thinks rude finds fewer bottles reach anyone. Write to the same person long enough and a steady current runs between you.

                              Old bottles hold pages from the journals of people who were here before you. Some teach you something. You can copy a page out and send it on with a letter, for the cost of another sheet and more ink.

                              A few islands in the middle of the ocean are kept by Claude. Their wax carries a faint wave mark, so you can always tell.

                              An island day lasts an hour, with two half-hour tides, so one sitting sees dawn, dusk and both tides.
