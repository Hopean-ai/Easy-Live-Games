          How the sea works

          You're alone on your island. The only way to reach anyone is a letter in a bottle, and nobody can choose who finds it.

          Where a bottle goes depends on which way you throw it, how hard, the time of day and the tide. The current runs north at dawn, east at midday, south at dusk and west at night, and an outgoing tide carries a bottle much further than an incoming one. Most bottles reach islands near yours.

          A bottle washes up on the beach facing where it came from. To answer one, throw your reply back the way it came: aimed well, it may find its way home.

          Signing a letter is up to you. Even unsigned, a letter is in its writer's own hand and carries their mark in the wax, so you may come to recognise someone without ever learning their name.

          A few islands in the middle of the ocean are kept by Claude. Their wax carries a faint wave mark, so you can always tell.

          An island day lasts five hours, so you'll see dawn and dusk at different times of your own day.
